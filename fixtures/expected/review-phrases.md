# Review packet: phrases and difficulty ratings

**Status: NOT YET REVIEWED BY A VIOLINIST.**

If you are a violin teacher or an advanced player, this document is the
ask. It should take about twenty minutes. Write straight into the blank
fields; nothing here is precious.

Source: `wohlfahrt-p3`, 61 measures, 15 phrases,
from a real scan of Wohlfahrt Op. 45 Book 1.

## What PracticeMap claims, and what it does not

**Claims:** that these numbers describe what each passage *demands*, read
from the printed notation, under a stated tempo, and that they are a
consistent ordering.

**Does not claim:** that the scale is validated, that the category labels
match any syllabus, or that a 6.8 here would mean the same in other
repertoire. The rubric also cannot see bowing beyond what is printed,
fingering, string choice, shifts, your hand, or your level.

The one external check available so far: Wohlfahrt printed these studies
in increasing order of difficulty, and the app agrees -- Etude 2 averages
4.0, Etude 3 averages 5.7. That is ordinal agreement with one editor on
one pair of studies, from 25 and 7 rated measures respectively. A real
signal, and a weak one.

## What would actually change the code

Disagreement is the useful outcome here. Three questions matter most, and
the last section says which constant each answer would move.

1. **Is the spread right?** Etude 2 sits at 4.0 and Etude 3 at 5.7. Does
   that 1.7-point gap match the difference you feel between them?
2. **Are the cut points right?** The scale breaks at 2.0 / 4.0 / 6.0 / 8.0
   into Beginner-friendly, Advanced Beginner, Competent, Expert and
   Extremely hard. Would you put these etudes in those bands?
3. **What is under-weighted?** Which demand does the rubric most obviously
   miss or under-count on this page?

Also worth marking as you go:

- Do the phrase boundaries fall where a musical idea actually ends?
- Is a boundary marked low-confidence one you would also hesitate over?
- Any measure marked 'Needs review' that is plainly legible on the page?

Boundary confidence is honest about thin evidence: etudes are often
continuous, with no rest or cadence to mark a phrase end, and a bare
barline is deliberately weak evidence.

## Where recognition fell short

- **8 measures were read and rejected.** Their note durations did not add up to the time signature, so they are left unrated rather than shown as a guess. If one of these is plainly legible to you, that is a recognition bug worth reporting.
- **21 measures were never read at all** because 12 transcription request(s) did not complete. This is not a judgement about the notation.
- **2 section(s) disagreed with the page** about how many measures they contained, and were left unrated rather than risk attaching notes to the wrong measure.

Full provider errors are kept in the analysis JSON, not here.

## Where your answers would land in the code

So this is not a survey that goes nowhere:

| If you said | What changes |
|---|---|
| The gap between the etudes is too small | The curve in `_saturate`, `src/features/difficulty/rubric.py`, which compresses the top of the scale |
| The gap is too large | The same curve, in the other direction |
| A specific demand is under-counted | Its entry in `WEIGHTS`, `src/features/difficulty/rubric.py` |
| A demand is missing entirely | A new feature in `measure_features`, plus a weight for it |
| The category bands are wrong | `CATEGORIES` in `src/features/difficulty/colors.py` |
| A phrase boundary is in the wrong place | The evidence weights in `WEIGHT`, `src/features/segmentation/phrases.py` |
| A legible measure was marked unreadable | Recognition rather than the rubric: `src/server/recognition/` |

Each of those constants is covered by tests that pin current behaviour, so a change made in response to this review will show exactly what else it moves.
